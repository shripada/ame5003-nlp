Lessons

Unit I — Text processing and information retrieval

Session 01 What NLP is, and your first tool Why language is hard for computers — and how to pull facts out of text with a pattern. Session 02 Capturing, choosing, and the greedy trap Regular expressions, part two: extracting the individual pieces of a match, handling more than one input format, and understanding greedy matching. Session 03 Tokenization How a computer splits text into words, and why that turns out to be a difficult problem. Session 04 Cleaning up text Normalization and stop words: deciding which differences in text do not matter and removing them. What counts as irrelevant depends on the task. Session 05 Stemming and lemmatization Reducing a word to a root form by two different methods, and deciding which one a given task requires. Session 06 Part-of-speech tagging Labelling each word with its grammatical job — and why the same word needs a different label depending on the words around it. Session 07 Named entity recognition Finding the names in text — people, places, companies — which is harder than tagging words, because a single name can span several of them. Session 08 Searching a collection The term-document incidence matrix and how Boolean queries are answered with it, together with the reason it does not scale to real collections. Session 09 The inverted index Keep only the ones. For each word, a list of the documents it appears in — and a fast way to answer a query by walking two lists at once. Session 10 Ranking results with TF-IDF Not just which documents match, but which match best — and why the score needs a logarithm in two different places.

Unit II — Language models, sentiment, word vectors

Session 11 Language models and n-grams A model that guesses the next word. It is the same idea ChatGPT runs on — and the simplest version of it is just looking at the last word or two. Session 12 N-gram probabilities and the Markov assumption Where the numbers come from. The exact answer is easy to write down and impossible to use — so we deliberately use the wrong one. Session 13 The zero problem and smoothing Counting says an unseen sentence is impossible. It is not. Fixing that means taking probability away from what we saw and giving it to what we did not. Session 14 Judging a language model: perplexity We have built three models and have no way to say which is best. The measure that settles it is a single number, but it means something only when computed on text the model has never seen. Session 15 Bayes' rule and the Naïve Bayes classifier The question we want to ask cannot be counted. So we turn it into two questions that can — and then tell two deliberate lies to make the second one countable too. Session 16 Naïve Bayes for sentiment, worked through Five short reviews, one new sentence, and every number done by hand. The training is nothing but counting — and the first honest attempt fails completely. Session 17 Bag of Words and the vector space model You built this table in session 8 and used it as a lookup. Change the 0s and 1s to counts, read the columns instead of the rows, and it becomes a map with documents as places on it. Session 18 The TF-IDF vector model Last session's table rewarded the wrong words. Today we re-weight it — and the answer we get back is not the one anybody expects. Session 19 Cosine similarity Last session left a number on the board and refused to explain it. Today we derive it — and then use it to get session 17's clustering back. Session 20 Neural networks in one hour Nothing in this session is usable on its own, which is deliberate. It supplies the machinery session 21 requires: five ideas, each selected because Word2Vec uses it. Session 21 Word2Vec: CBOW and Skip-gram Last session ended with a sentence nobody could have read an hour earlier. Today we build the thing it describes — and find out that the prediction task was never the point. Session 22 GloVe One loss function and one co-occurrence matrix. What makes the session worth an hour is the question it raises about Word2Vec, and the answer, which is not the one GloVe's own paper gives.

Unit III — Deep learning and applications

Session 23 The RNN: hidden state, shared weights A feedforward layer needs an input of one fixed size, and sentences are not all one size. An RNN runs the same small network once per word instead, carrying a running summary — the hidden state — forward from one word to the next. Session 24 Training an RNN: backprop through time, and the vanishing gradient Last session ended on a promise the architecture makes and a question nobody answered. This session answers it, and the answer is no — not by default. That "no" is the entire reason the next session exists.

Labs

Unit I — Text processing and information retrieval

Lab 01 · Week 1 Refresher and setup Get Colab working, and refresh the Python this course leans on. Lab 02 · Week 2 Information extraction with regular expressions Pull amounts, dates and accounts out of messy bank SMS — and find where regex stops working. Lab 03 · Week 3 Tokenize, normalize, stem, remove stop words The standard preprocessing steps — and when each one quietly destroys your meaning. Lab 04 · Week 4 Incidence matrix and inverted index Build a tiny search engine two ways, and see why the inverted index is the one that scales.

Unit II — Language models, sentiment, word vectors

Lab 05 · Week 5 N-grams and n-gram probabilities Build a language model by counting, watch one unseen pair score a good sentence at zero, then make it write English. Lab 06 · Week 6 Sentiment analysis with Naïve Bayes Sentiment with Naïve Bayes — from the lecture toy to 2,000 reviews, then measure it honestly. Lab 07 · Week 7 Cosine similarity and TF-IDF Cosine and TF-IDF: finish an exercise the textbook skips, find out why scikit-learn disagrees with the lecture, and build a search engine. Lab 08 · Week 8 Word2Vec, GloVe, and the comparison Train Word2Vec, download GloVe, and measure — for real — whether either beats TF-IDF at sentiment.
