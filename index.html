Lessons

Unit I — Text processing and information retrieval

Session 01 What NLP is, and your first tool Why language is hard for computers — and how to pull facts out of text with a pattern. Session 02 Capturing, choosing, and the greedy trap Regular expressions, part two — pulling the pieces out, and the one bug everybody writes at least once. Session 03 Tokenization How a computer splits text into words — and why that turns out to be a hard problem. Session 04 Cleaning up text Normalization and stop words — deciding which differences in text do not matter, and erasing them. The catch is that "does not matter" depends on the job. Session 05 Stemming and lemmatization Cutting a word down to a root form — the fast crude way, and the slow correct way — and knowing which one your task needs. Session 06 Part-of-speech tagging Labelling each word with its grammatical job — and why the same word needs a different label depending on the words around it. Session 07 Named entity recognition Finding the names in text — people, places, companies — which is harder than tagging words, because a single name can span several of them. Session 08 Searching a collection The term-document incidence matrix, and answering Boolean queries with it — a clean idea that works, right up until it does not fit in memory. Session 09 The inverted index Keep only the ones. For each word, a list of the documents it appears in — and a fast way to answer a query by walking two lists at once. Session 10 Ranking results with TF-IDF Not just which documents match, but which match best — and why the score needs a logarithm in two different places.

Unit II — Language models, sentiment, word vectors

Session 11 Language models and n-grams A model that guesses the next word. It is the same idea ChatGPT runs on — and the simplest version of it is just looking at the last word or two. Session 12 N-gram probabilities and the Markov assumption Where the numbers come from. The exact answer is easy to write down and impossible to use — so we deliberately use the wrong one. Session 13 The zero problem and smoothing Counting says an unseen sentence is impossible. It is not. Fixing that means taking probability away from what we saw and giving it to what we did not. Session 14 Judging a language model: perplexity We have built three models and cannot say which is best. The fix is one number — but it only means anything if you measure it on text the model has never seen. Session 15 Bayes' rule and the Naïve Bayes classifier The question we want to ask cannot be counted. So we turn it into two questions that can — and then tell two deliberate lies to make the second one countable too.
