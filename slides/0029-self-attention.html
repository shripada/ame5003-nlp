Self-attention: query, key, value, and the dk

Session 28's three operations, pointed at one sequence instead of two. The arithmetic barely changes. What changes is that the recurrence is left with nothing to do — and that a set of vectors compared pairwise has no notion of word order at all.

Session 28's two sequences were different sequences: a decoder state asked the question and the encoder states answered it.

In self-attention, each position of a sequence attends to positions of that same sequence. Nothing in score–softmax–weighted-average changes. What changes is what is being compared to what.

SLP3's opening example: The chicken didn't cross the road because it was too tired

Computing the representation for it, "the tokens chicken and road both have a high attention weight… since it will end up coreferring with either chicken or road."

This is the job session 23's recurrence was doing — build a representation of a word that depends on its context.

The RNN carried context forward one step at a time through ht. Self-attention fetches it directly, in one operation, from wherever in the sentence it happens to be.

Three names, introduced as three roles one input vector plays

A query is what a position uses to ask. A key is what it offers to be matched against. A value is what it contributes once matched.

Every position plays all three in one layer: query when its own output is being computed, key and value when everybody else's is.

Each role gets its own learned projection

qi=xiWQki=xiWKvi=xiWV(8.9)

WQ and WK are both [d×dk] — a query and a key get dot-producted together, so they must have the same dimensionality.

WV is [d×dv], and dv is free. Nothing dots a value against anything.

Session 25 opened on one vector asked to do more than one job: a plain RNN's ht is the memory, the output and the next input at once. Its answer was to split the vector.

Self-attention reaches the same diagnosis about xi and answers it differently: project the vector three ways instead of splitting it. The projections are learned, so what a position advertises as a key need not be what it hands over as a value.

The middle three are session 28's three, unchanged

  • The score compares projections instead of raw states. And it is a parameterised score: qi⋅kj=xiWQ(WK)𝖳xj𝖳 is (13.38)'s bilinear form with Ws=WQ(WK)𝖳. Session 28's table already had a row for this
  • The score is divided by dk. That is the next section

(8.14) is new rather than different: WO reshapes the head's output back to width d, so the layer can be stacked. It does no attending, and it matters far more in session 30.

Chapter 8 develops self-attention for a left-to-right language model, so every equation restricts attention to the current position and earlier ones. A model trained to predict the next word must not be allowed to look at it.

That restriction is causal masking, it is not part of the mechanism, and session 31 teaches it once encoder and decoder blocks are separated. For now: the sum runs over j≤i because chapter 8's model is a decoder.

The chapter says the scaling exists because "the result of a dot product can be an arbitrarily large value, and exponentiating large values can lead to numerical issues and loss of gradients during training".

That explains why some division is wanted. It does not explain why the divisor is dk rather than dk, or log⁡dk, or 10.

The derivation, and it is two sentences

"Assume that the components of q and k are independent random variables with mean 0 and variance 1. Then their dot product, q⋅k=∑iqiki, has mean 0 and variance dk."

A dot product of two dk-dimensional vectors is a sum of dk independent terms; variances of independent terms add. Variance dk means a standard deviation of dk.

So the typical size of a score grows like dk, and dividing by dk puts it back to about 1 whatever the dimension. The divisor is the standard deviation of the thing being divided.

dk=64 is the original transformer's value, so the middle row is the case that matters: without the division, scores reach the softmax spread over about ±8 instead of ±1.

e8≈2981 against e−8≈0.0003. The largest score takes essentially all the weight and every other position is switched off.

That is a saturated softmax, and its real cost is the gradient: a weight already at 0.9999 has almost no derivative, so nothing about the scores can be learned any more. The scaling does not change what the softmax computes — it keeps its input in the range where it still distinguishes things.

The objection was correct and the promise was that this session would divide. But the two divisions are not the same division.

Cosine divides by the actual lengths of the two vectors in front of it. dk divides by the expected scale of a score at this dimensionality — one constant, identical for every pair. A key with a large norm still scores highly against everything. What keeps that from being fatal is layer normalisation, in session 31.

Exercise

Take WQ=WK=WV=I, so qi=ki=vi=xi
x1=(1,0)x2=(0,1)x3=(1,1)

Compute α2j and head2, with dk=2 so dk=1.4142, attending over all three. Then: what happens to those weights if all three inputs are multiplied by 10?

Raw dot products with q2=(0,1): 0, 1, 1. Divided by 1.4142 the scaled scores are 0.0000, 0.7071, 0.7071, and the softmax gives 0.1978, 0.4011, 0.4011.

head2=0.1978(1,0)+0.4011(0,1)+0.4011(1,1)=(0.5989,0.8022)

Positions 2 and 3 tie, as they should. Position 1 is orthogonal to the query and still gets weight 0.198 — a score of zero is not a weight of zero, because e0=1.

Times 10: both query and keys grew, so the dot products grow by 100 — 0, 100, 100, scaled to 0, 70.71, 70.71. The weights become 9.8×10−32, 0.5, 0.5. One of the three inputs has been erased from the output entirely, and nothing was masked; the softmax saturated on its own. All information about how much more relevant the survivors were is flattened too.

Scaling inputs by 10 is not something that happens on purpose. Increasing dk is, and by footnote 4 it has the same effect for the same reason. Note the word delay: dividing by a constant slows the growth, it does not abolish it.

Sessions 25 and 26 closed on the same limitation: ht cannot be computed until ht−1 exists.

Self-attention has no such dependency. Every ai depends only on the xs and never on any other a — so all n outputs can be computed at once.

Pack the N embeddings into X, one token per row

equationSLP3 eq.
all projections at once Q=XWQK=XWKV=XWV(8.32)
the whole head head=softmax(mask(QK𝖳/dk))V (8.33)
output projectionA=headWO(8.34)

QK𝖳 is N×N, and its (i,j) entry is qi⋅kj — every score in the layer, in one multiplication.

SLP3 gives N for real language models as commonly 1K to 32K tokens. That is the scale at which "one matrix multiply rather than N sequential steps" stops being an aesthetic preference.

It is also, unavoidably, an N×N matrix — so the cost is quadratic in sequence length. That is the price this design pays.

One sentence attending to itself — equation (8.33) after mask and softmax

Every row is a distribution. The eleven cells of the it row sum to exactly 1, which is why the first row — with only one cell available — is at full strength.

The reach is one step, not n. The it row puts 0.33 on chicken and 0.27 on road, six and two positions back, without passing through anything in between. The weights shown are genuine row softmaxes over scores chosen to match SLP3's Figure 8.3, not the output of a trained model.
Question 1 of 2

What distinguishes self-attention from the attention taught in session 28?

Correct: one sequence attending to itself — which is what the word self is doing in the name.

The first needs care, because its conclusion is right and its reasoning is wrong. Self-attention is faster, but it has more parameters, not fewer: WQ, WK, WV and WO are all new. The speed comes from parallelism, not from size. The third has it backwards — the softmax is still there, and the scaling happens inside it.

What self-attention gives up

(8.13) is a weighted sum of values whose weights come from dot products between pairs. Nothing in that expression refers to where anything sits.

Shuffle the input tokens and the same set of pairs gives the same dot products, and every output is the same as before — attached to a different position, otherwise untouched.

Self-attention treats its input as a set of vectors. The chicken crossed the road and the road crossed the chicken are the same object to it.

The causal mask conceals this a little, since j≤i at least knows which positions come earlier — but knowing that j precedes i is a long way from knowing that j is three words before i.

Session 27 opened by arguing that translation is not word-by-word substitution: the green witch arrived becomes llegó la bruja verde, an alignment of 4, 1, 3, 2. Word order differs between languages and has to be handled.

The recurrence handled order for free — it was the one thing an RNN could not fail to represent, because it read the words in order. This session threw the recurrence away, and order went with it.
Question 2 of 2

Why is the dot product in equation (8.11) divided by dk?

Correct: the variance argument — Vaswani's footnote 4. At dk=64 that is scores spread over roughly ±8, which the exercise showed is enough to collapse a softmax onto one position.

The second is the likeliest error: non-negativity and summing to 1 are the softmax's doing, and hold whatever the scores are. The scaling changes how sharp the distribution is, never whether it is a distribution. The fourth describes cosine, which divides by actual lengths.

Summary

Session 28's mechanism pointed at one sequence, with each input projected into three roles — session 25's diagnosis repaired by projection instead of splitting. The score is scaled by dk because the dot product has variance dk, verified by simulation.

No output depends on any other, so n sequential steps become one multiply and any two positions are one dot product apart. The cost is quadratic in length — and worse, a set of vectors compared pairwise carries no word order at all. Session 30 puts order back.
