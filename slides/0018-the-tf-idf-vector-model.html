Term frequency — unchanged from session 10

tf={1+log10⁡(count)when count>00when count=0

Inverse document frequency — also unchanged

idf=log10⁡(Ndf)

A word in every document has df=N, so idf=log10⁡(1)=0.

The value is exactly zero rather than merely small, and any quantity multiplied by exactly zero is zero. This property is used throughout the session.

Exercise

The largest count anywhere in the table

good occurs 114 times in As You Like It, and it appears in all 37 plays. What is its tf-idf weight?

tf=1+log10⁡(114)=3.057, a healthy weight. idf=log10⁡(37/37)=0. So tf-idf=3.057×0=0.

Would a larger count have helped? If good occurred 10,000 times then tf=5.0, and 5.0×0 is still 0.

No term frequency can compensate for an idf of zero. Session 17's exercise concluded good should be dropped; TF-IDF drops it automatically, and exactly rather than approximately.

Multiply the two, cell by cell

tf-idf=tf×idf=(1+log10⁡count)×log10⁡(Ndf)

The obvious reading is that TF-IDF failed

That reading is wrong, and the reason matters more than the arithmetic does.

idf is a property of the collection, not of the document.

Nothing about As You Like It changed. We asked how distinctive its words are among 37 Shakespeare plays — and Shakespeare uses fool in 36 of them and wit in 34. Within his collected works those words are close to universal.

fool and wit are genuinely good words for telling a comedy from a history in general. In a collection of all English books they would carry real signal.

In a collection consisting entirely of Shakespeare they carry none. The words did not change; the collection did.

TF-IDF did not break the example; it measured it.

And the measurement says these four words cannot distinguish Shakespeare's plays, because Shakespeare uses them in nearly all of them. That is a fact about the collection we did not have before.

The idf table already holds the answer

  • Romeo, idf 1.57, in exactly one play — it identifies that play alone
  • Falstaff, idf 0.966, in four — it groups the Henry plays and nothing else
  • forest, idf 0.489, in twelve, largely the pastoral comedies

These separate the plays cleanly because they are rare in this collection. Our four words were chosen in session 17 because raw counts made them look valuable.

Weighting the matrix has established that our features were poorly chosen, and established it from the numbers rather than by judgement.

A method that reports that the chosen features are poor for a collection is working correctly, not failing.
Question 1 of 2

To tell Shakespeare's 37 plays apart, which of these four words is the best feature, and why?

Correct: Falstaff, idf 0.966.

Appearing in only 4 plays is exactly what makes it useful — observing it eliminates 33 of 37 immediately. The other three all reason from coverage, which is the opposite of what a discriminating feature needs.

Question 2 of 2

You take As You Like It, unchanged, and score it inside a collection of ten thousand modern novels instead of Shakespeare's 37 plays. What happens to its tf-idf vector?

Correct: every weight changes.

The tf values do not change — those are counts inside an unaltered document. Every idf does, because df and N both come from the collection. fool and wit would go from near-zero to informative, and good would stop being zero as soon as one novel omitted it.

A tf-idf vector is not a description of a document. It is a description of a document relative to a collection.

One more problem, unrelated to weighting

Take Julius Caesar's raw column and imagine the same text printed twice. Every count doubles.

The same play, twice over

d=[7,62,1,2]vector length 62.432d=[14,124,2,4]vector length 124.87

The Euclidean distance between them is 62.43 — where the largest distance in the whole weighted matrix was 0.521.

By this measure the two documents are wildly different. They are the same play, printed twice.

Doubling a vector does not move it, it lengthens it. Both point in exactly the same direction; one is simply longer.

What we want to compare is direction, not magnitude. There is a standard measure with exactly that property, and on these two vectors it returns 1.0000. Session 19 derives it.

Summary

TF-IDF weights each cell so common words shrink and distinctive words dominate, and a word in every document collapses to exactly zero. But what counts as distinctive is measured against a particular collection.

When our four Shakespeare words nearly all collapsed, that was not a failure but a report that those words describe this collection poorly.
